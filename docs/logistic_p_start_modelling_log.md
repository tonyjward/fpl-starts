# Logistic P(start): modelling log

A record of the decisions behind `logistic_availability_v1`, in the order
they were taken, with the reason for each and the numbers that came out.
This is the audit trail. The public walkthrough is
`notebooks/logistic_p_start_model.ipynb`, and the operational reference
(commands, outputs, rules) is `docs/logistic_p_start_model.md`.

The specification was chosen in an earlier exploratory feature analysis.
The numbers in this log come from the public panel built in this
repository, from the files under
`models/logistic_availability/logistic_availability_v1/` and from the
prediction snapshots.

## 1. Goal

Build a P(start) model that is **strong, calibrated and genuinely
interpretable**, and can be published as part of this repository. It is
*not* meant to get as close as possible to the private LightGBM model.
Every prediction has to be explainable in plain language by a later
LangGraph layer, so it must store the exact arithmetic behind it.

Consequences:

- Logistic regression. Each input becomes a signed log-odds contribution,
  so "why is this player's probability high or low" can be read directly
  from the stored numbers.
- A new public feature set instead of a copy of the private one.
- The public historical story is **naive baseline → interpretable
  logistic**. `raw_lookup` and `refined_availability` are not presented
  as competing production models.

## 2. Candidate features and the collinearity problem

Many candidate predictors measure almost the same thing: starts, start
rates and minutes over 2, 4, 6, 10 and 20 gameweeks, this season and last,
with pairwise rank correlations of 0.85–0.998. Keeping several of them
makes individual coefficients unstable and can make them misleading. For
example, a "started last week" coefficient can turn negative once minutes
are also in the model.

The trade-off, measured with the walk-forward folds of section 7: a model
with every candidate at once (31 coefficients, generated by
`fpl-starts-logistic-case-study`) against the compact model (15).

| | All candidates | Compact (v1) |
|---|---|---|
| Brier | 0.07608 | 0.07825 |
| Rotation Brier | 0.15180 | 0.15574 |
| Calibration slope | 0.986 | 0.961 |
| Share of the improvement over the naive baseline | 100% | 92.1% (Rotation 90.2%) |
| Largest VIF | infinite (exact linear dependencies) | 4.0 |
| Coefficients changing by more than 30% between folds | 14 of 31 | 0 of 15 |
| Median fold-to-fold change, 15 shared columns | 47% | 6% |

The larger model predicts better: 0.0022 lower Brier overall (2.9%) and
0.0039 in the Rotation stratum (2.6%). It pays for that with severe
multicollinearity and coefficients that contradict football sense: being
flagged doubtful (75%) or injured *raises* the log-odds of starting,
`starts_last_4` is negative, and the "started last week" flag is negative.

**Decision:** one predictor per football concept. The compact model keeps
about 92% of the larger model's improvement over the naive baseline, with
substantially more stable coefficients that each mean something on their
own. Do not keep searching large numbers of feature combinations: that
trade-off is the point of the model.

## 3. `last_gw_role` + `minutes_prior_3_gws`, not `minutes_last_4`

The two leading options for recent form were:

- `last_gw_role` (a category for what happened last gameweek) plus
  `minutes_last_4`: minutes over the last four gameweeks, *including*
  last week;
- `last_gw_role` plus `minutes_prior_3_gws`: minutes in the three
  gameweeks *before* last.

Everything else is identical: the same other five predictors, 15
coefficients plus an intercept in both, and the same walk-forward folds,
with preprocessing and C chosen inside each fold (section 7).

| Spec | Window | Brier | Rotation Brier | Cal. slope | ECE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `minutes_prior_3_gws` | overall | 0.07825 | 0.15574 | 0.961 | 0.0175 |
| | 2024-25 | 0.08247 | 0.15266 | 0.920 | 0.0209 |
| | 2025-26 | 0.07438 | 0.15850 | 1.007 | 0.0148 |
| `minutes_last_4` | overall | 0.07785 | 0.15464 | 0.966 | 0.0168 |
| | 2024-25 | 0.08204 | 0.15153 | 0.928 | 0.0208 |
| | 2025-26 | 0.07400 | 0.15743 | 1.008 | 0.0148 |

`minutes_prior_3_gws` costs 0.0004 Brier overall (0.5% relative, and
consistent across both test seasons) and 0.0011 in the Rotation stratum
(0.7%). The exploratory analysis put the gap at 0.0002–0.0004. Calibration
is effectively the same. C was 3 in both folds for `minutes_prior_3_gws`,
and 1 then 3 for `minutes_last_4`, where the validation curves are flat.

Neither option has a coefficient that changes sign between folds. The
difference is in what the coefficients mean. `minutes_last_4` overlaps
with last week's minutes (Spearman 0.86), so last week's effect is split
between two terms and the `last_gw_role` coefficients shrink:

| Coefficient (fold 2024-25 / 2025-26) | with `minutes_prior_3_gws` | with `minutes_last_4` |
|---|---|---|
| `started_60_plus` | 2.40 / 2.30 | 1.94 / 1.84 |
| `started_under_60` | 0.71 / 0.81 | 0.44 / 0.54 |

Dropping `last_gw_role` and keeping `minutes_last_4` on its own is clearly
worse: 0.0827 Brier overall and 0.1659 in the Rotation stratum.

**Decision:** use `last_gw_role` + `minutes_prior_3_gws`. The two do not
overlap, so an explanation can keep three things apart without counting
last week twice:

- what happened in the immediately previous gameweek;
- playing time in the three gameweeks before that;
- longer-term current-season role (`current_season_start_rate`).

That interpretability is worth more than a few ten-thousandths of Brier,
and must not be optimised away.

### Remaining collinearity

The chosen predictors are not uncorrelated, and cannot be: regular
starters score high on all of the form and role features. Measured on the
86,755 training rows with the frozen preprocessing:

| Pair | Spearman |
|---|---|
| `minutes_prior_3_gws` / `current_season_start_rate` | 0.82 |
| `last_gw_role` (ordered) / `minutes_prior_3_gws` | 0.78 |
| `last_gw_role` (ordered) / `current_season_start_rate` | 0.75 |
| `previous_season_start_rate` / the three above | 0.50–0.65 |

Variance inflation factors:

| Column | VIF with `minutes_prior_3_gws` | VIF with `minutes_last_4` |
|---|---|---|
| minutes window | 3.68 | 5.90 |
| `last_gw_role__started_60_plus` | 3.22 | 4.42 |
| `current_season_start_rate` | 4.02 | 4.01 |
| every other column | 1.0–1.4 | 1.0–1.4 |

Every VIF in the chosen specification is below 5, no coefficient changes
sign between folds, and none of the form or role coefficients moves by
more than 0.17 between them. (The rare availability levels move more,
up to 0.59 for `suspended`, because they have few rows, not because of
collinearity: their VIFs are about 1.)
This is moderate collinearity, unlike the 0.85–0.998 overlap among the
rejected candidates (section 2). Removing it entirely would mean dropping
a concept, such as season-long role, or replacing features with
residualised versions, both of which make the coefficients harder to
explain. We keep it.

## 4. The frozen specification

Six conceptual predictors, fixed in `src/fpl_starts/ml/spec.py`:

1. `availability_status`: categorical, reference `available`
2. `last_gw_role`: categorical, reference `did_not_play`
3. `minutes_prior_3_gws`: continuous
4. `current_season_start_rate`: continuous
5. `previous_season_start_rate` (continuous) + `no_previous_season` (flag)
6. `first_game_at_club`: flag

The category levels are readable names and are fixed in code rather than
learned from data, so the model matrix is identical in every fold, fit and
prediction. In total there are 15 coefficients plus an intercept.

Any change to `spec.py` is a new model: it gets a new `MODEL_ID` and is
never an edit to `logistic_availability_v1`.

## 5. Preprocessing

- Continuous variables are standardised with the training mean and SD.
- A missing continuous value is replaced by the training mean, so it
  transforms to 0 and contributes nothing.
- **No generic missingness indicators.** The only flags are the two with a
  football meaning, `no_previous_season` and `first_game_at_club`, and
  they carry the "no history" effect.
- Binary variables stay 0/1 and unscaled. Categorical variables are
  dummy-coded against an explicit reference level.
- L2 logistic regression (scikit-learn, `lbfgs`).
- Every preprocessing statistic is fitted on training rows only, **inside
  each historical validation fold** (`evaluate.run` refits it per fold).

## 6. Temporal boundary

| Seasons | Role | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2022-23 | history/context only, never a training row | first season of the panel |
| 2023-24 – 2025-26 | model development and training | used to choose the specification |
| 2026-27 | untouched prospective evaluation | the evidence that matters most |

**2022-23 is context only.** It is the first season in the available
panel. Its rows have no previous-season history, and early in the season
every player looks like a new arrival at his club. Those are artefacts of
where the data starts, not football, and including those rows noticeably
distorted the coefficients and calibration. It still supplies
previous-season rates and recent form for 2023-24's features. This is a
decision about where the data starts, made independently of any 2026-27
result. It is not cherry-picking.

**2024-25 and 2025-26 are development data.** Both were looked at while
choosing the feature specification, so neither is described anywhere as an
untouched test set.

**From the freeze onward**, no feature, preprocessing, calibration or
regularisation choice may use 2026-27 outcomes. The frozen model is never
refitted, retuned or recalibrated on 2026-27. An improved model would be a
new model id with its own prospective record, starting from when it was
introduced.

Leakage rules in the panel (`src/fpl_starts/ml/panel.py`):

- Features for gameweek N use only gameweeks strictly before N.
- Availability is the latest snapshot strictly before N's cutoff
  (deadline − 2h). Anything later is rejected.
- Both fixtures of a double gameweek share one feature vector.
- Form features reset on a confirmed change of club. Previous-season role
  carries across a transfer.

Walk-forward by season (`fpl-starts-logistic-evaluate`):

| Test season | Trained on | C (chosen inside fold) |
|---|---|---|
| 2024-25 | 2023-24 (C validated on its last 25% of gameweeks) | 3.0 |
| 2025-26 | 2023-24 + 2024-25 (C validated on 2024-25) | 3.0 |

The benchmark is naive P(start | started previous gameweek at this club),
fitted on the same training window.

| Window | Model | Brier | Rotation Brier | Cal. slope | ECE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| overall | naive | 0.1038 | 0.1920 | 0.98 | 0.003 |
| overall | logistic | **0.0783** | **0.1557** | 0.96 | 0.018 |
| 2024-25 | naive | 0.1097 | 0.1939 | 0.94 | 0.013 |
| 2024-25 | logistic | 0.0825 | 0.1527 | 0.92 | 0.021 |
| 2025-26 | naive | 0.0983 | 0.1903 | 1.03 | 0.006 |
| 2025-26 | logistic | 0.0744 | 0.1585 | 1.01 | 0.015 |

Relative Brier improvement over the baseline: **24.6% overall (18.9%
Rotation)**, 24.9% in 2024-25 (21.3% Rotation) and 24.4% in 2025-26 (16.7%
Rotation). The Rotation stratum is players who started 15–50% of the
season's gameweeks so far, the group where P(start) matters most for
decisions. Strata follow `fpl_starts.scoring.label_strata` and are
evaluation labels only, never model inputs.

The naive baseline has the lower ECE. It is a two-cell lookup table, so it
is close to perfectly calibrated by construction but cannot tell players
apart. The logistic model's calibration slope stays close to 1.

## 8. The frozen model

Fitted by `fpl-starts-logistic-train` on 2026-09-24 at 16:40 UTC.

- Training rows: 86,755 player-fixtures (29,725 in 2023-24, 27,283 in
  2024-25 and 29,747 in 2025-26). The training start rate is 28.9%.
- C was chosen by training on 2023-24 + 2024-25 and validating on 2025-26,
  then the model was refitted on all three seasons. The validation Brier
  curve is flat above C = 1 (0.07441 at C = 1 and 0.07437 at C = 10), so
  in practice the choice of C does not matter. **C = 10.** It is the
  largest value in `spec.C_GRID` (0.003–10), but the curve moves by only
  0.00001 between C = 3 and C = 10, so a wider grid would not change the
  model materially. The walk-forward folds in section 7 choose C = 3
  because each validates on a different, earlier window.
- `model.json` records the input-file hashes, a hash of the training
  matrix, a hash of the model source and the library versions
  (scikit-learn 1.9.1, pandas 3.0.5, numpy 2.5.3). It was fitted from
  uncommitted code on top of `972b9cc`. The source hash identifies that
  exact code.

Intercept −1.911. That is the reference player: available, did not play
last gameweek, average on every continuous feature, has a previous season
and is not new at his club.

| Feature | Coefficient |
|---|---|
| status `doubtful_75` | −0.84 |
| status `doubtful_50` | −1.73 |
| status `doubtful_25` | −3.49 |
| status `unknown` | −3.22 |
| status `injured` | −4.57 |
| status `suspended` | −5.66 |
| status `unavailable` | −5.99 |
| last GW `sub_appearance` | +1.17 |
| last GW `started_under_60` | +0.90 |
| last GW `started_60_plus` | +2.36 |
| `minutes_prior_3_gws` (per SD ≈ 105 min) | +0.47 |
| `current_season_start_rate` (per SD ≈ 0.36) | +0.75 |
| `previous_season_start_rate` (per SD) | +0.19 |
| `no_previous_season` | −0.16 |
| `first_game_at_club` | +1.18 |

Notes for anyone explaining these coefficients:

- The availability levels are ordered as football intuition expects, and
  the doubtful effect roughly doubles at each step from 75 to 50 to 25.
- `sub_appearance` (+1.17) is slightly above `started_under_60` (+0.90).
  A start that ended early often means an injury or a tactical withdrawal,
  and the season-rate features already carry much of the "regular
  starter" signal. Neither is a mistake, but an explanation should not
  say that being subbed on "counts for more than starting".
- `first_game_at_club` is positive because it acts alongside other
  features. A new signing's form features fall back to the mean, and his
  `last_gw_role` sits at the did-not-play reference. The flag offsets that
  default, so it should be explained together with those values, not on
  its own.

## 9. Prediction and explainability

`fpl-starts-logistic-predict` loads `model.json` and never fits anything.
Each run writes a write-once snapshot,
`predictions/2026-27/gwNN_logistic_availability_<timestamp>.json`. It uses
the same format as the other models, so `fpl-starts-derive` and
`fpl-starts-score` handle it. Per player it adds:

- every model column: `feature`, `raw_feature`, `raw_value`,
  `transformed_value`, `coefficient` and `contribution`;
- `intercept`, `logit` and `p_start`;
- the model id and `model.json` hash, the prediction cutoff, and
  `generated_after_deadline`.

`model.json` carries a plain-language `descriptions` entry for each column.
From these, the explanation layer can build sentences such as "he started
and played 60+ minutes last week, which strongly raises his predicted
chance of starting", while the stored numbers stay exact.

GWs 1–5 were scored after the fact. Their inputs are strictly pre-cutoff
and the model never saw 2026-27, but they were not real-time forecasts,
and the snapshots say so. GW6 onward are true prospective forecasts. GW1's
lower total (~9.7 expected starters per club) comes from new signings and
missing current-season history at the start of the season.

Every snapshot is kept, so predictions can later be compared with actual
starts without hindsight.

**Forecast cutoff, from 2026-09-27.** Forecasts now use availability
captured at any time before the deadline, instead of before deadline − 2h,
so late news counts. Only forecasting changed (`ml/predict.py`): the
fitted model, `spec.py` and the historical training data are unchanged,
and the training cutoff stays deadline − 2h. Training availability
snapshots were typically ~9.5h before the deadline (median), so the model
already saw inputs of varying freshness. The snapshots above used
deadline − 2h; each snapshot records its own `prediction_cutoff`. Because
`predict.py` changed, `model_source_sha256` in `model.json` describes the
code as fitted, not the current forecasting code.
